Hour & Sign

Three cards from a full 78 card deck, cut by your birth date and the planetary hour you ask in. The deck reshuffles eight times a day, so the same question gets a different answer after dark.

New to this? Start here

Nothing here is a prediction. It is a prompt, a structured way of asking yourself a question, which is roughly what tarot has always been for. Read it the way you read a horoscope in a newspaper, with your tongue somewhere near your cheek.

The deck
All 78 cards. The 22 Major Arcana are the big thematic ones: The Fool, Death, The Star. The 56 Minor Arcana are four suits of everyday life. Wands for drive, Cups for feeling, Swords for thought, Pentacles for money and the body.
Reversed
A card dealt upside down. It usually softens, blocks or inverts the usual meaning rather than making it bad. You can turn reversals off in the reading options.
Your standing card
One Major worked out from your birth date and yours for life. It never changes. Readers call this a birth or personality card.
Planetary hour
An old system giving every hour of the day a ruling planet, cycling in a fixed order. It is why Sunday is the Sun's day and Monday is the Moon's.
The frightening looking cards
Death, The Tower, The Devil and the Three of Swords alarm nearly everyone on a first reading. None of them is literal. Death is change, The Tower is sudden clarity, The Devil is the habit you know you are in, and the Three of Swords is ordinary heartbreak. Nothing has been removed from this deck to spare you.
The three positions
The Hour, The Sign and The Turning are this page's own names, not traditional ones. A standard three card spread is usually Past, Present and Future, or Situation, Action and Outcome. What is traditional here is the card meanings, the reversals, the elemental dignities and the birth card arithmetic, which is Mary Greer's method. The spread itself is particular to this deck, because it is built around the hour rather than around a story.
Asking twice
You get one card per question, once a day. Dealing again and again on the same question until the answer improves is the one thing every reader will tell you not to do, so the page simply will not let you. You may draw a single clarifier, which is allowed, and then that question is closed until tomorrow.
How exact is any of this?
Not very, and deliberately so. Sunrise is treated as six in the morning, because the page never asks where you are. The rising sign uses equal two hour houses rather than a real ephemeris. The moon is an average month, and Mercury's retrograde is modelled rather than looked up, so both can be a day or two out at the edges. That is plenty for a card a day. Reading options

Reversals change the orientation of a card, never which cards appear. Weighting lets the sky lean on the shuffle, using the old correspondences between the planets, the signs and the Major Arcana, so the card of the hour's ruling planet comes up a little more often. Turn it off and the deck is perfectly flat. Either way, no card is ever excluded.
