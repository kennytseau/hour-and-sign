Hour & Sign

Three cards from a full 78-card deck, cut by your birth date and the planetary hour you ask in. The deck reshuffles eight times a day — the same question gets a different answer after dark.

New to this? Start here

Nothing here is a prediction. It is a prompt — a structured way of asking yourself a question, which is roughly what tarot has always been for. Read it the way you read a horoscope in a newspaper: with your tongue somewhere near your cheek.

The deck
All 78 cards. The 22 Major Arcana are the big thematic ones — The Fool, Death, The Star. The 56 Minor Arcana are four suits of everyday life: Wands (drive), Cups (feeling), Swords (thought), Pentacles (money and body).
Reversed
A card dealt upside down. It usually softens, blocks or inverts the usual meaning rather than making it "bad". You can turn reversals off below.
Your standing card
One Major worked out from your birth date and yours for life. It never changes. Readers call this a birth or personality card.
Planetary hour
An old system giving every hour of the day a ruling planet, cycling in a fixed order. It is why Sunday is the Sun's day and Monday is the Moon's.
The frightening-looking cards
Death, The Tower, The Devil and the Three of Swords alarm nearly everyone on a first reading. None of them is literal. They are about endings, sudden clarity, habits and plain old heartbreak — and each says so on the card itself. Nothing has been removed from this deck to spare you.

The odds, and what is bending them

A flat deck gives every card the same chance. This one does not, when weighting is on: traditional correspondences raise some cards' odds for this exact hour and date. Nothing is ever excluded — the worst-weighted card still gets dealt. Here is precisely what is applied right now.

How this draw was made

No server and nothing stored. The daily cards come from a seeded pseudo-random generator, so the same inputs always produce the same spread — refresh as often as you like, it will not budge until the hour turns. The one-question card above is the opposite: it uses the browser's cryptographic randomness and is different every single time.
